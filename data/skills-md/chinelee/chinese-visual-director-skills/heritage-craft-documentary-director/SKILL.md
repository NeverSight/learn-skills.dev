# Heritage Craft Documentary Director

Build documentary images that make the work understandable. Hands, tools, material state, sequence, and workshop context matter more than generic portraits of a “master.”

## Modes

- **Process still:** show one verifiable operation and material transformation.
- **Maker portrait:** place identity within actual work, tools, and environment.
- **Apprenticeship:** show demonstration, observation, correction, or shared work without staged hierarchy.
- **Object lineage:** connect raw material, process evidence, finished object, and use.
- **Series:** document preparation, transformation, inspection, and completion.
- **Reference-led/repair:** preserve authorized makers, tools, workshop, and craft facts.
- **Direct image:** generate only when explicitly requested.

## Select a route

Read [references/visual-system.md](references/visual-system.md) before writing.

- `hand-process` for cutting, shaping, carving, weaving, throwing, firing, painting, polishing, brewing, or assembly.
- `maker-in-workshop` for environmental portraiture grounded in actual practice.
- `teaching-and-transfer` for apprenticeship, family transmission, class, or collaborative correction.
- `material-to-object` for raw, intermediate, finished, and used states.

If the craft is unspecified or unfamiliar, ask for the technique or state uncertainty. Do not guess culturally specific procedures.

## Lock documentary facts

- craft name, region, community, and heritage status only when verified;
- maker identity and authorization;
- process stage, material state before/after, tool name, grip, contact point, and work surface;
- workshop layout, protective equipment, heat, dust, water, ventilation, and safety;
- clothing actually appropriate to the work;
- final object's function and ownership;
- commercial purpose: archive, editorial, exhibition, education, or brand story;
- ratio and whether face or hand detail is primary.

## Direction workflow

1. State the exact process stage and physical transformation.
2. Map hand, tool, material, force direction, and support surface.
3. Keep workshop evidence necessary to understand the work.
4. Choose face, hands, object, or environment as the primary evidence layer.
5. Use available daylight and practical work light plausibly.
6. Describe dust, steam, fibers, chips, slip, dye, heat, or wear only when process-caused.
7. Add dignity through attention and agency, not heroic myth.
8. Compile prompt, uncertainty, and nearest negative constraints.
9. Audit facts, safety, sequence, anatomy, tools, and cultural framing.

## Default output

1. `纪录事实与未知项`.
2. `工序和证据重点`.
3. `ImageGen 中文提示词`.
4. `负面约束`.
5. `纪录组图` only when requested.

Read [references/reference-and-output.md](references/reference-and-output.md) for references, series, identity, platform output, and repair.

## Boundaries

- Do not label a craft or maker as officially recognized intangible heritage without evidence.
- Do not invent ethnic dress, ritual, lineage, poverty, hardship, age, or biography.
- Do not replace real tools with picturesque antiques or unsafe substitutes.
- Do not stage hands in impossible positions or hide the actual operation behind smoke and shallow focus.
- Do not romanticize the maker as vanishing, primitive, or timeless.
- Do not turn every craft image into a smiling portrait or product advertisement.
- Generate images only on explicit request.
