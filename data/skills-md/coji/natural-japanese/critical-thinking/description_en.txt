The skill of reviewing and correcting business documents (such as approval documents, proposal papers, plan documents, reports, etc., documents that ask the reader to make a judgment) from the decision‑maker’s perspective. If you are asked to “review and correct from the decision‑maker’s viewpoint,” “look at it before it’s submitted,” “make it acceptable,” or “check the approval (proposal/plan),” follow the procedure “review and correct from the decision‑maker’s viewpoint.” Do not ask for answers that can be found within the document; instead, correct it, ask up to three questions that only the author can answer, insert those answers into the main text, and place any verification items in a separate file outside the document. This is the basic entry point.

When the process is divided into steps, there are three cases. If you are only asked to check for contradictions, verify the basis, and see whether the logic is weak, follow the **logic** procedure. If you are asked whether the premises are correct, whether there are alternative options, and what would overturn the argument, follow the **somosomo** procedure (in both cases, write nothing in the document; return the numbered questions and answers in YAML). 

After the inspection, if you are told “revise by reflecting the comments,” follow the “revise by reflecting the comments” procedure; if you are told “re‑structure from the outline” or “use a different construction,” follow the “re‑structure from the outline” procedure (retain the facts and requested content, replace only the argumentative backbone, produce a structural draft and let the author choose). 

If you are asked for “more concise” or “more polite,” follow the “more concise / more polite” procedure (determine the length of the main text by the depth of the logic’s YAML; preserve the facts and decision items). Do not use this for requests to make Japanese sound natural or easier to read (that is the job of natural‑Japanese).
