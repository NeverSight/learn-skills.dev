Skills for writing and revising Japanese business documents so that they are easy to read and understand. Includes creation and proofreading of business documents such as minutes (including conversion from transcripts), research reports, analysis reports, internal guides and manuals, research memos, discussion papers, project proposals, proposals, reports, emails, and slide outlines, used for any instructions like “write from the conclusion,” “make the argument clear,” “make headings concise,” or “explain technical terms in an understandable way.” Removal of AI‑like tone (direct, indirect, or colloquial remarks such as “AI‑like,” “AI‑smelling,” “machine‑translated feel,” “unnatural,” “more natural Japanese,” “machine‑like,” “make it sound human,” “monotonous,” phrases like “it can be said that …,” as well as being told or suspected that the text was written by AI). Requests to improve difficult‑to‑read or unclear sentences (awkward word order, overly long sentences, unclear intent, misplaced commas, etc.). New writing of note articles, blog posts, essays (including requests to write from scratch on any given topic), rewriting and polishing existing texts. Diagnosing and scoring AI‑like tone (requests such as “Did AI write this?,” “Give a score for AI‑ness,” “Determine how AI‑like it is” that do not involve rewriting). Also accommodates requests to have the system learn one’s writing style or create a profile (including requests to read past writings and produce text in one’s own style). Addresses removal of prohibited words, monotony of rhythm, uniformity of paragraph structure, literal‑translation feel from English syntax, as well as readability principles such as word order, comma placement, one idea per sentence, and subject‑predicate distance. Formatting of technical document sections and Markdown (e.g., one sentence per line, block quotes, footnote syntax) is out of scope—those belong to a different skill area; this skill focuses solely on the naturalness, readability, and clarity of the text.
