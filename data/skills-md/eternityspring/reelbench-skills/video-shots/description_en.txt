Shot breakdown: split a finished video into a per‑shot analysis table—duration, shot type, category, camera movement, and visual composition of each shot.

The division of labor is ingrained: **the "energy" is measured by code volume** (cut points come from ffmpeg scene detection, duration is the difference between cut points, motion amount is the median of per‑frame differences). The model only judges the few things it truly needs to judge (shot type / category / camera movement / composition / rhythm), and each judgment is immediately verified by the code—**it may claim pans, tilts, shakes, but measurements show almost no movement, so it gets blocked outright**.

Watching the footage follows a contact sheet (for each shot, the first frame and the last frame are stitched into a large image; one screen shows dozens of shots, a/b comparison reveals camera movement), not frame‑by‑frame scrolling. Missed or extra cuts are detected; use recut to insert or delete cuts, automatically renumber and recompute durations; manually adjusting boundaries will fail the gate.

Output shots.json + a Markdown shot list + **a single‑page interactive breakdown report**: embedded player (synchronizes highlighting of shots during playback, click a shot to jump), shot rhythm strip, searchable, filterable, sortable shot table (list and card views, start and end keyframes side‑by‑side, click thumbnail to enlarge), distribution of shot types, categories, camera movements, appearing characters, quality gates, export JSON. Single file with zero dependencies, can be opened offline with a double‑click.

All 15 quality gates are deterministically checked by scripts.

Zero dependencies, zero API keys; only node and ffmpeg are required.

Use when asked to do a shot breakdown, split shots, analyze video shots, shot durations, shot types, camera movements, shot lists, video shot breakdown, shot list from video.
