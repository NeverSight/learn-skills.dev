# Naming and Address Engineering

## Use when

The referent stays the same; the name changes. A naming act reclassifies the
object and with it the active social relation, emotional distance, and
narrative alignment.

## Do not use when

Do not hunt for "more interesting" names. Do not vary names for stylistic
variety — unearned renaming blurs reference. If the frame has not changed,
the name should not change either.

## Core test

**Who names, and why is this identity the one activated now?**

Every name is a classification. 那个男人, 老头, 店主, 李叔, 父亲, 主任,
病人, 嫌疑人 — the same body, eight different worlds. The question is never
"which word is richer" but "whose act is this naming, and what does it
declare?"

## What a naming act carries

- **knowledge state** — what the speaker knows about the referent (病人 vs
  嫌疑人 is an epistemic claim);
- **social relation** — 李叔 vs 主任 activates kinship or institution;
- **emotional distance** — 父亲 vs 那个男人 places the speaker near or far;
- **institutional role** — 主任, 病人, 嫌疑人 summon a role-bound world;
- **current relevance** — which identity matters for this scene's decision;
- **ingroup/outgroup status** — 我们的人 vs 那个外来的;
- **narrative alignment** — whose perception the narration has adopted.

## Workflow

1. Mark every naming act for the referent across the span.
2. Ask who the speaker/perceiver is and what they know right now.
3. Ask why this identity is the relevant one for the current decision or
   perception.
4. Test the frame switch: replace the name with another legal name and read
   what the relation model becomes.
5. Switch names only when the frame actually changed — knowledge, relation,
   distance, or relevance moved.
6. Check continuity: an unmarked shift to a new name is itself a signal
   (often a deliberate one); mark it as such or repair it.

## Minimal pair

> 狗在门口等着。
>
> 阿黄在门口等着。
>
> 那畜生还在门口。

The same animal. The first is a neutral object, the second names a family
member, the third declares the speaker's war. No psychological description
is needed — the narrator's position is entirely in the naming.

> 父亲把钥匙放在桌上。
>
> 那个男人把钥匙放在桌上。

If the daughter is the perceiver and she has stopped calling him 父亲, the
second is not decoration: the estrangement is the event. If the narrator is
neutral, the second may be a continuity break.

## Counterexamples

- A child may call the same adult 叔叔, 爸爸, and 那个人 in one scene as the
  relation shifts mid-scene; the switches are the psychology.
- A formal register may require titles even when intimacy exists (主任 in
  front of colleagues); the name is role performance, not distance.
- Deliberate misnaming (a character refusing to use a new title) is a
  conflict in miniature; do not "fix" it.

## Rule strength

**Framing comparison.** No preferred naming density, no synonym lists, no
"vary your references" rule. The reliable operations are: enumerate the
legal names, compare the frames each activates, attribute the naming act,
and test the switch as a one-variable intervention (see
`counterfactual-revision`).

before the new name.
