# Narrator Intervention and Abstraction Control

## Use when

The narration itself is doing something the scene has not authorized: naming
an emotion the reader could infer, stating what a scene "means" before the
scene has produced it, summarizing a relationship in a register no present
perceiver would use, or keeping the reader at a level of abstraction where
nothing can be checked.

## Do not use when

Do not enforce "show, don't tell." A direct state label can be the cheapest
truthful sentence — 他很累 after a long action sequence is exact, and adding
a catalogue of drooping eyelids dilutes it. Do not replace one cliché with
皱眉/攥拳/呼吸一滞 merely because these are visible. Do not punish explicit
abstraction in essays, law, technical writing, or a character whose job and
situation genuinely call for it. Sometimes the narrator should conclude.

## Core test

Two questions, in order:

**Who owns this sentence?** Swap the nouns. If the sentence still fits a
quarterly review, therapy recap, or generic life-advice post, a foreign
register has entered the narration. The sentence may be replacing an event
with an administrative summary of the event.

**What does the reader need to know right now?** If the answer is a checkable
fact, offer evidence with a bounded domain: several nearby readings remain
legal, irrelevant readings do not. Specificity is not detail count — one
well-chosen event can constrain interpretation more than five facial
features.

## Workflow

1. Mark the smallest sentence that sounds externally summarized, or that
   names a state/meaning directly.
2. Ask who owns it: character, narrator, institution, or nobody in
   particular.
3. Underline abstract relation or development claims: "realized," "meant,"
   "relationship," "growth," "new stage," "in a sense."
4. Recover the scene fact those claims summarize.
5. Keep the abstract sentence only if it adds information unavailable from
   the scene or belongs to the speaker's authentic register.
6. Otherwise delete it, or replace the label with one bounded piece of
   evidence: an object, action, timing, threshold, or measure that carries
   the difference.
7. Check the evidence's domain: does it admit a small family of plausible
   readings rather than one sealed diagnosis or unlimited vagueness?

## Intervention scale

Narrator presence is a dial, not a defect:

- **scene** — only what a present perceiver can register;
- **near scene** — inference just behind the perceiver's shoulder;
- **summary** — the narrator compresses a span;
- **verdict** — the narrator states what it all means.

The same passage can move along the dial. The failure is not being high on
the dial; it is being high **without the scene having paid for it**, or
settling meaning before the scene earns it.

## Minimal pair

> 她把钥匙放回桌上。她意识到，他们的关系已经发生了变化。
>
> 她把钥匙放回桌上。这次没有拿走。

The second does not become "more human" by using concrete nouns. It stops
charging the narrator to explain a relation the altered action can carry.
If later context still makes the breakup unmistakable, the abstraction was
redundant; if not, the test reveals missing evidence rather than proving
abstraction is bad.

Evidence pair:

> 她显得很不耐烦。
>
> 她看了第三次表。

The second does not "show" impatience by rule. It supplies a timed action
that supports impatience, anxiety, urgency, or calculation while excluding
many unrelated states. Context chooses among the nearby readings.

## Counterexamples

- "他意识到煤气没关。" Keep it: the realization is an event with immediate
  informational content.
- A management consultant narrating a deliberately comic breakup as
  "关系进入维护期": keep it if the register is the joke and belongs to the
  character.
- "他很累。" may be exact after a long action sequence; the label is
  cheaper than the evidence it would duplicate.
- A deliberately essayistic narrator may run high on the intervention dial
  as their whole voice; do not flatten a working aesthetic into scene prose.

## Rule strength

**Discourse tests.** No abstraction quota, no show-don't-tell law, no
ban on emotion labels. The reliable operations are ownership attribution
(whose register is this), conclusion timing (has the scene earned this
verdict), and evidence bounding (does the evidence admit the right small
set of readings). Each is testable as a one-variable intervention (see
`counterfactual-revision`).

## Return shape

For analysis, return **span -> owner -> scene fact -> keep/delete/rewrite
reason**. For revision, change only the sentences whose ownership or
explanatory load fails; offer one or two evidence candidates, not an
inventory of gestures. Do not sand the whole passage into neutral prose.
