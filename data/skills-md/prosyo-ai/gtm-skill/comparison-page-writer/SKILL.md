Use this for "<competitor> vs <you>", "<competitor> alternatives" and "best <category> for <use case>" pages. Readers on these pages are close to buying; honesty converts them better than spin.

## Inputs

- Competitor research (current pricing with the date checked, features, positioning, reviews).
- Your product facts, pricing and limits.
- Proof: customers who switched, migration stories, review quotes you may use.

## The play

### 1. Pick the page type
| Query | Page | Promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "x vs y" | Head-to-head | Help me choose between these two |
| "x alternatives" | List of options, you included | Help me find something better than x |
| "best <category> for <use case>" | Ranked list | Help me shortlist |

### 2. Lead with the verdict
A short answer at the top: who should choose the competitor, who should choose you. Readers and answer engines both pull from it.

### 3. Build the criteria table
Criteria the buyer cares about (not just your strengths): price and pricing model, setup time, key capabilities, limits, integrations, support, best for. Mark facts with a "last checked" date.

### 4. Write the sections
1. Quick verdict.
2. At-a-glance table.
3. Key differences, each with what it means for the buyer.
4. Pricing compared, with a worked example for a typical team.
5. When the competitor is the better choice (yes, really).
6. Switching: effort, what moves over, help offered.
7. FAQ (5-8 questions).
8. CTA that fits: try free, see a migration plan, book a comparison demo.

### 5. Add SEO and schema
Title under 60 characters ("X vs Y: honest comparison for <audience>"), meta under 155, FAQPage schema, internal links to the other comparison pages and the product pages.

## What good looks like

- A reader who should choose the competitor would agree with the page.
- Every factual claim about the competitor is current and checkable.
- The pricing example uses a realistic team, not a cherry-picked one.
- The common mistake is a page where you win every row, which readers distrust and reviewers call out.

## Rules

- MUST date pricing and feature facts about competitors.
- MUST include a "when they are the better choice" section.
- NEVER misstate a competitor's features, prices or limits.
- NEVER use a competitor's logo or trademarks in ways that imply endorsement.

## Example prompts

- "Write a 'Rotaflow vs us' page for multi-location dental groups."
- "Write a 'Top 7 Rotaflow alternatives' page that includes us honestly."
- "Refresh our comparison page pricing table and FAQ."
