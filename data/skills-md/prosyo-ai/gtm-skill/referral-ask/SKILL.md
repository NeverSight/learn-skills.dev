Use this to turn goodwill into introductions. Most people are happy to refer; they just need a specific ask and an easy way to do it.

## Inputs

- Customers or contacts who are happy (recent results, good feedback, renewals).
- Target accounts or roles you want to reach, and any known connections.
- Recent wins to reference.

## The play

### 1. Ask at the right moment
Right after a visible win: a result they shared, a renewal, a great review, a successful rollout, a thank-you message from them. Not during an open support issue.

### 2. Make the ask specific
"Do you know anyone who might need this?" gets "I'll think about it." Instead, name two or three companies or a precise role: "I noticed you're connected to Dev at Lumen and know the ops team at Fabrikam. Would either be open to a short intro?"

### 3. Use a double opt-in
Ask the referrer to check with their contact first, so the introduction is welcome. Offer to send a forwardable blurb.

### 4. Write the forwardable blurb
Three or four sentences the referrer can paste: who you are, what you did for them, why it might matter to the contact, and an easy next step. Written in the referrer's voice, short enough to forward from a phone.

> Dev, thought of you: Sam at Acme helped us cut month-end close from 9 days to 4. Given your new entities at Lumen, might be worth 20 minutes. Happy to connect you if useful.

### 5. Follow up and thank
Reply to the introduction within hours, move the referrer to bcc, and tell them what happened afterwards. Thank them whatever the outcome; a handwritten note or a small, appropriate gesture works. Follow any rules their company has about gifts, and disclose referral rewards.

## Templates

**The ask**
> Hi Priya, really glad the 4-day close held through quarter end. One favour: you mentioned knowing the finance lead at Fabrikam. Would they be open to an intro? I've drafted a short note below you could forward if they are. No pressure at all if it's not a fit.

**Thank-you after an intro**
> Priya, thank you for connecting me with Lena. We've got a call next week. I'll let you know how it goes, and I owe you one.

## What good looks like

- Asks name specific people or companies.
- The referrer spends under a minute.
- Every referrer hears back about the outcome.
- The common mistake is a quarterly mass email asking all customers for "any referrals", which almost never produces introductions.

## Rules

- MUST use double opt-in introductions.
- MUST disclose any referral reward and respect the referrer's company policies.
- NEVER contact someone claiming a referral that has not happened.
- NEVER ask a customer with an open problem for a referral.

## Example prompts

- "Priya just renewed and shared great results. Write a referral ask for two specific companies."
- "Write a forwardable blurb for our customers to send to peers."
- "Design a simple customer referral program for a B2B SaaS."
