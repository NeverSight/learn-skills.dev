Use this when a competitor comes up often on calls. A battlecard is the on-call version of a teardown: short enough to read in 30 seconds with the buyer still talking.

## Inputs

- Competitor research (or run a teardown first).
- Win-loss notes against this competitor.
- Your differentiators and approved proof.

## The play

### 1. Lead with when you win and lose
The most useful line on the card. Reps need to know whether to fight or qualify out.

### 2. Write the quick positioning
Two sentences a rep can say when the buyer mentions the competitor, acknowledging their strength and stating your difference fairly.

### 3. Three differentiators with proof
Only differences that matter to buyers and that you can prove. Each: what, why it matters to the buyer, proof.

### 4. Landmine questions
Questions the rep asks that lead the buyer to discover the competitor's weakness on their own. Fair, neutral, answerable.

### 5. Objection responses
"They're cheaper", "They have feature X", "We already use them for Y". One or two lines each.

### 6. Traps
What the competitor says about you and how to answer it; what not to do (trash talk, matching their discount without a trade).

Read [references/battlecard-template.md](references/battlecard-template.md) for the full one-page template and a filled example.

## What good looks like

- Fits on one screen; a rep can find an answer mid-call.
- Fair about the competitor's strengths; buyers trust reps who are.
- Updated each quarter with fresh win-loss notes and pricing.
- The common mistake is a ten-page feature comparison that reps never open on a call.

## Rules

- MUST keep claims about the competitor factual, current and sourced.
- MUST include when to walk away.
- NEVER disparage the competitor or its people.
- NEVER use confidential competitor information.

## Example prompts

- "Make a battlecard against Acme for our mid-market reps."
- "Turn this teardown into a one-page battlecard."
- "What should I say when a prospect says 'Acme is half the price'?"
