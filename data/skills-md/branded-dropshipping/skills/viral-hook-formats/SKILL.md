# Viral hook formats

An opening is an offer: a few seconds of the viewer's time in exchange for something they can recognize wanting, from someone they have a reason to believe. Make that offer in words, image and action together, then make the next beat start paying it. Work from the user's actual subject, speaker, footage, product facts and intended action. An opening can invite learning, participation, recognition, suspense or enjoyment; it need not always provoke shock or describe pain.

Return the requested artifact. **Hooks-only means hooks only.** A small edit stays small. Preserve selected wording and fixed footage; explain a material mismatch without silently replacing a settled choice. Use adequate supplied context rather than starting a new research campaign. If a missing fact prevents a personal claim, use a supported alternative or ask for that fact—not a whole new intake form.

## Compose and deliver

Start with what the intended viewer can recognize and what they might want next. Pick a concrete entry into the material. Draft the opening together with its first continuation, even when only the opening will be returned; this catches promises the body cannot fulfill. Check how the first frame and overlay change the spoken invitation. A result preview can support a method explanation and undermine an unresolved challenge.

Use the speaker's truthful language and material. A read-through can expose awkward phrasing or missing connections; it is not a compulsory review loop or proof of vocal performance. Preserve humor, companionship and expressive personality rather than adding every retention device. Do not fabricate personal experience, stakes, testimonials, rankings, study results or precise product benefits.

Worked examples in the references are composed illustrations; none reports a measured result. Use the user's existing notes or a short table when multiple versions need tracking. Return only the requested artifact and reasoning, without an unsolicited self-grade or performance promise.

## Inspection tools and boundaries

Use an available browser for live inspection; see [browser access](references/browser-runtime-and-sessions.md) if needed. Local writing needs no browser.

For caption preparation, read [caption reading and observation](references/caption-normalization-and-observation.md). For local frame/clip preparation, read [media inspection](references/media-inspection-contract.md). Use available subtitle, editor or media-inspection tools and check their actual interfaces. These operations prepare evidence. A screenshot is not continuous viewing, transcription is not listening, and clicking Play does not establish that the model received audio/video. State exactly what was inspected.

Keep public lookups to minimum public identifiers or generic terms. Do not submit the private brief, adapted copy or internal findings to search, page chat, transcription or other downstream services. Supplied media enters the selected assistant's ordinary dataflow; local extraction alone does not make the whole analysis offline. Extra uploads require understood, authorized recipients and scope.

Full filming, generation and editing execution belongs with creative production; this skill supplies opening decisions and usable handoffs. Product/offer evidence comes from its owner. Meta campaign budgets, attribution and live tests belong with campaign management. Consume approved destination links without rewriting referral parameters. No publishing, spending, outreach or commerce changes follow from a hook-writing task. The name describes the craft territory, not a guarantee of reach or sales.
