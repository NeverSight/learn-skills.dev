# Comment writing

Write comments that sound like a thoughtful teammate: direct, human, collaborative, and easy to act on. Adapt the voice and detail to the audience and channel without changing the facts.

## Before drafting

Identify:

- **Audience:** who needs to understand or act on the comment.
- **Purpose:** update, explanation, request, decision, handoff, or follow-up.
- **Known facts:** what was observed, changed, verified, and what remains unknown.
- **Action:** what the reader is being asked to do, if anything.
- **Channel constraints:** mentions, formatting, length, language, or required fields.

If a missing fact would make the comment misleading or change its ask, clarify before drafting. Otherwise make a narrow, transparent assumption.

## Writing principles

- Lead with the point that matters to the reader.
- Use short paragraphs and ordinary language. Prefer a few clear sentences over a long block or unnecessary bullets.
- Say what happened, what was done, what was verified, and what happens next. Include only the parts relevant to the audience.
- Make requests specific, polite, and easy to answer. Use the user's language and the team's normal level of formality.
- Keep separate topics in separate paragraphs. Avoid jargon and English terms when a natural local-language equivalent is clearer.
- Preserve important scope boundaries, caveats, and dependencies. Do not make a future task sound completed.
- Do not claim that a change, test, deployment, approval, or outcome happened unless there is evidence for it. Distinguish “configured” from “verified,” and state what remains pending.
- Do not invent names, dates, owners, causes, commitments, or results. Omit unnecessary sensitive details.
- Use mentions only when the channel supports a real mention and the intended person is identified. Do not add decorative or duplicate mentions.

## Suggested structure

Adapt this order rather than forcing every section:

1. A brief greeting when appropriate.
2. What was found or why the comment is being made.
3. What was done and what evidence confirms it.
4. The next step, open dependency, or clear request.

For a short status comment, one or two compact paragraphs are usually enough. Use bullets only when the reader needs to scan several independent items.

## Drafting and publishing

- When asked to **draft**, return the complete proposed text without publishing it.
- When asked to **publish**, use the authorized channel and its required formatting. Respect any mandatory approval, mention, or security workflow for that channel.
- If publication is not explicitly authorized, do not post, send, or otherwise create an external side effect. Ask for approval after showing the draft when required.
- Before publishing, verify that the final text still matches the approved draft and that the target, recipients, mentions, and requested action are correct.

## Final check

Before handing over a comment, confirm:

- It is accurate and does not overstate verification.
- The reader can tell what happened and what is expected next.
- The tone is respectful and natural for the channel.
- It has no unnecessary detail, duplicate content, or sensitive information.
- Any approval or publication boundary has been followed.
