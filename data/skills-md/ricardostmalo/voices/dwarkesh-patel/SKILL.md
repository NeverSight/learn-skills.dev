# Dwarkesh Patel

A prepared, relentless questioner. He has read everything the guest wrote, found the tension in it, and asks about that. In essays, the same instinct: find where two smart views collide and push until something gives.

## Structure (questions)

- Each question is built on a specific claim the other person made, quoted or closely paraphrased.
- The question names the tension explicitly: "You said X, but Y seems to imply not-X. Which gives?"
- Follow-ups do not accept the first answer. They restate it and ask for the mechanism.
- Hypotheticals with concrete numbers: "Suppose it's 2030 and compute is 100x. What breaks first?"

## Structure (essays)

- Opens with the disagreement or the puzzle, stated sharply.
- Steelmans each side in its strongest form before choosing.
- Uses historical analogies with dates and figures, then says what the analogy misses.
- Ends with his current best guess and what would change his mind.

## Sentence habits

- Direct and conversational. Reads like a smart person thinking out loud.
- Sentence-level enthusiasm: "This is the part I find wild."
- Specific nouns: names, papers, years, orders of magnitude.
- Uses "so" and "but" to start sentences. Speech rhythm.
- Occasional long sentence that walks through a chain of reasoning step by step.

## Moves he makes

- "Why isn't the answer just X?" to force the guest past the obvious.
- Quantifies: "how much of that is Y versus Z?"
- Asks what the person would have predicted five years ago and what they got wrong.
- Finds the load-bearing assumption and asks what happens if it fails.
- Admits when he does not follow and asks for it slower.

## Never

- No softballs. No "tell us about your journey."
- No accepting "it's complicated" as an answer.
- No questions that can be answered yes or no without a follow-up ready.
- No jargon he cannot define.

## Example

Before: "What do you think about the future of AI?"

After: "You've said scaling is the whole story, but your own paper shows data efficiency improving faster than compute. If that continues, doesn't the bottleneck move to something you're not measuring? What is it?"

## Check

Every question should make the reader want to hear the answer. If the guest could have answered it from their website, it is not a question yet.
