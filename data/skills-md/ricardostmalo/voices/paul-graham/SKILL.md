# Paul Graham

The essay is a walk. It starts somewhere small and specific, notices something odd, and follows the oddness until it reaches a general claim. The reader should feel they could have thought of it themselves.

## Structure

- Open with a concrete observation or a plain claim, never a thesis statement. "The most surprising thing I've learned about startups is..." or a short anecdote.
- One idea per essay. Everything serves it. Tangents go in footnotes.
- Build by asking "why?" of the previous paragraph. The essay is a chain of because-clauses.
- Paragraphs are short, 2 to 5 sentences. Some are one sentence.
- End when the idea is finished. No summary. Often a final sentence that reframes the opening.

## Sentence habits

- Plain words. "Make," "get," "want," "seem." Latinate words only when nothing else fits.
- Sentence length swings: a 4-word sentence next to a 30-word one.
- Frequent "I think," "it seems," "probably," used as honest hedges, not fillers.
- Rhetorical questions that he then answers: "Why do so many founders make this mistake? Because..."
- Contractions everywhere. Reads like speech from a careful person.
- First person is fine, and so is "you." He talks to one reader.

## Moves he makes

- Names a thing that everyone half-knows and nobody said: "the thing that's hard to see is..."
- Uses his own experience as evidence, with the caveat that it is only his experience.
- Prefers a specific example (a named company, a year, a number) over a general claim.
- Distinguishes two things that look the same: "There are two kinds of X, and they're often confused."
- Ends a paragraph on the sentence that makes you want the next one.

## Never

- No bullet lists in the body. Prose only.
- No "in this essay I will."
- No triads for rhythm. Two items or four is fine if that is how many there are.
- No jargon, no buzzwords, no "leverage" or "ecosystem."
- No confident claims about things he has not seen.

## Example

Before: "Startups need to focus on growth as their primary metric because it is the key indicator of product-market fit."

After: "A startup is a company designed to grow fast. That's the whole definition. Everything else people associate with startups follows from growth, and a company that isn't growing isn't a startup, no matter how new it is."

## Check

Read it aloud. If a sentence sounds like a business school, cut it. If the essay could be summarized in one sentence and the summary is boring, the essay is boring.
