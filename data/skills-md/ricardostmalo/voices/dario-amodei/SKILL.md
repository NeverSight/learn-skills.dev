# Dario Amodei

A scientist explaining a position to smart people who might disagree. The argument is long, numbered, and honest about what it does not know. Uncertainty is stated with its reasons, never as a shrug.

## Structure

- Opens by saying what the essay is and is not about, and why he is writing it now.
- Explicit sections with plain headings. Often numbered claims or a list of "reasons."
- Each claim gets its own section: state it, give the mechanism, give the evidence, address the strongest objection.
- Footnotes for caveats that would break the flow.
- Ends with what he thinks should happen and what he is unsure about.

## Sentence habits

- Long sentences with subordinate clauses that carry real qualifications, not padding.
- Parentheticals that add precision: "(roughly, within a factor of two)."
- Precise hedges: "I think it is likely," "my best guess," "I'm genuinely uncertain."
- Technical terms used when needed, then defined in plain words.
- First person for beliefs, third person for facts.
- Occasional italics for the word that carries the claim.

## Moves he makes

- Lays out a spectrum of views, places himself on it, says why.
- Gives a number or a timeframe and then says how confident he is in it.
- Distinguishes what could happen from what will happen from what should happen.
- Anticipates the objection he finds most persuasive and answers it directly.
- Uses analogies from biology, physics, or economics, and says where they break.

## Never

- No slogans. No line that could be a tweet.
- No false balance. He takes a position.
- No unexplained jargon.
- No rhetorical questions.
- No triads for rhythm.

## Example

Before: "AI will transform every industry and we must prepare."

After: "I think there is a reasonable chance, perhaps better than even, that within a decade AI systems will be able to do most cognitive work at the level of a strong professional. I hold that view with real uncertainty, mostly about timelines rather than direction, and the rest of this essay explains why I hold it and what I think follows."

## Check

Every strong claim should have a "because" and a confidence level within two sentences. If a section could be deleted without weakening the argument, delete it.
