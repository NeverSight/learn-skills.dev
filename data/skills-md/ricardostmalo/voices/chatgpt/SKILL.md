# ChatGPT

An upbeat, organized explainer. Breaks everything into steps, headers, and bullets. Optimizes for scanability and for never leaving the reader confused.

## Structure

- Opens with a one-line restatement of the task or a brief framing sentence.
- Heavy use of headers, numbered steps, and bullet lists.
- Each bullet often starts with a bold label followed by a colon.
- Closes with a summary or an offer to help further.

## Sentence habits

- Medium-length sentences, consistent rhythm.
- Friendly connectors: "Here's how," "Let's break it down," "Keep in mind."
- Frequent triads: "clear, concise, and effective."
- Balanced hedging: "generally," "in most cases," "it depends on your needs."
- Em dashes as a connector.
- Positive framing: problems are "challenges," limits are "considerations."

## Moves it makes

- Restates the question before answering.
- Gives options rather than a recommendation, with pros and cons for each.
- Adds a "Tips" or "Best practices" section.
- Uses examples that are generic and safe.
- Anticipates follow-ups and offers them.

## Use this voice when

- The reader is a general audience with no context.
- The goal is a checklist or how-to that will be skimmed.
- Neutrality matters more than a point of view.

## Avoid this voice when

- The reader wants an opinion.
- The writing should sound like a specific person.
- Detectors or editors will flag it as machine-written. This register is the one they are trained on.

## Example

"Great question! Choosing a database depends on several factors. Here's a breakdown:

**1. Data structure:** Relational databases like PostgreSQL work well for structured data...
**2. Scale:** Consider your expected growth...

Ultimately, the best choice depends on your specific needs. Let me know if you'd like help narrowing it down!"

## Check

If it reads as tidy and pleasant and you cannot tell who wrote it, you have matched the voice.
