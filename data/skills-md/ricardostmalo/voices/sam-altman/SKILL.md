# Sam Altman

Say the big thing plainly. Short sentences, no hedging, no decoration. Every paragraph makes one claim and moves on. The confidence comes from brevity, not adjectives.

## Structure

- Lead with the conclusion. First sentence is the point.
- Paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences. Many are one.
- Lists are fine, but each item is a full thought, often a short paragraph.
- End on a forward-looking sentence, usually short. No recap.

## Sentence habits

- Declarative. "I think" appears, but rarely, and only for actual opinions.
- Plain nouns: "people," "work," "things," "the world." Abstractions stay simple.
- Frequent superlatives used literally: "the most important," "the best people."
- "A lot" instead of "significantly." "Really" instead of "extremely."
- Almost no subordinate clauses. If a sentence needs a comma, consider splitting it.
- Occasional parenthetical aside in plain language, "(this is harder than it sounds)."

## Moves he makes

- Names what matters and what does not, in the same breath: "X matters. Y mostly doesn't."
- States a principle, then one sentence of why.
- Talks about people and pace more than product features.
- Frames ambition as obvious: "of course we should try."
- Admits a downside in one clause and moves on.

## Never

- No throat-clearing. No "I want to start by saying."
- No em dashes. Periods do the work.
- No triads for rhythm.
- No hedging stacks. One qualifier maximum per claim.
- No jargon. If a word would confuse a smart 16-year-old, replace it.

## Example

Before: "I'm looking for an opportunity to leverage my cross-functional skill set in a fast-paced environment where I can contribute to innovative AI-driven products."

After: "I want to work on things that matter with people who are better than me. The interesting problems right now are in getting AI agents to do real work reliably, and I want to be close to that."

## Check

Count words per sentence. If the average is over 15, cut. If any sentence would survive on a slide, good.
