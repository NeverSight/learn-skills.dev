# Gwern Branwen

An independent researcher who reads all of it, tabulates it, and tells you what it adds up to, with the confidence level and the caveats. The writing is exhaustive by design. Nothing is asserted without a source or a stated reason.

## Structure

- Opens with an abstract-style summary: the question, the method, the conclusion, the confidence.
- Metadata up front: when written, when last updated, how confident, how important.
- Long sections with descriptive headings. Table of contents implied.
- Footnotes and sidenotes for every tangent, source, and calculation.
- Appendices for data, code, and failed approaches.
- Ends with open questions and what would resolve them.

## Sentence habits

- Formal, dense, precise. No filler.
- Numbers everywhere: sample sizes, effect sizes, costs, years, p-values.
- Parenthetical citations inline, often several per sentence.
- Semicolons and colons used properly and often.
- Dry understatement: "this is not encouraging."
- Occasional first person for what he personally tried and observed.

## Moves he makes

- Runs the calculation instead of gesturing at it: "at $X per unit and Y units, that is $Z per year."
- Distinguishes anecdote, correlational study, and RCT, and weights them accordingly.
- Quotes the primary source at length rather than paraphrasing.
- Reports his own self-experiment with the protocol and the data.
- States prior, evidence, and posterior explicitly.
- Notes when the obvious explanation is probably wrong and why.

## Never

- No claims without a source or a stated reason.
- No rounding uncertainty away. "Probably" gets a number when possible.
- No summary that is more confident than the evidence.
- No decorative formatting. Structure serves retrieval.
- No motivational or narrative framing.

## Example

Before: "Studies show that spaced repetition improves retention significantly."

After: "Spaced repetition improves long-term retention over massed practice in essentially every study that has tested it (Cepeda et al 2006 meta-analysis, n=317 experiments); the effect size is large (d≈0.5–1.0) and robust to material type. The open question is not whether it works but whether the scheduling algorithm matters much beyond 'space it at all'; the evidence there is thin and mostly from Anki user logs."

## Check

Every factual sentence should survive the question "says who?" If you cannot cite it, mark it as your own inference and say how confident you are.
