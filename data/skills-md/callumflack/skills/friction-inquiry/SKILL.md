# Friction inquiry

Help the user understand what is bothering them and why it matters before deciding what to do. The first output is a working understanding for the user.

Treat friction as something worth investigating. Its intensity does not prove or disprove the account. Preserve the source's pressure, concrete claims, and distinctions. Do not soothe, moralize, diagnose, litigate tone, or require a polished account.

Use evidence to examine a reading of the problem. A concrete incident may be enough. A persuasive artifact or changed decision may come later.

## When to use it

Use this skill when the user wants to process an account into understanding. The issue may concern a product decision, technical judgment, ownership, recurring agent behavior, a relationship, or several connected problems. Anger is not required.

An irritated request to fix a known bug or execute a clear correction still calls for that work. Do not insert this workflow merely because the user sounds angry. If the user asks to understand recurring friction with an agent, examine that collaboration without recasting it as an interpersonal dispute.

## Take the account as it comes

Accept a voice recording, transcript, dictated account, rough note, or existing conversation. If the user only invokes the skill, invite them to talk or write through what happened without organizing it first. Offer a starting point if useful:

> What happened, what feels wrong, and what keeps coming back to you?

If the account is already present, work from it before asking questions. Use available audio or transcription tools when needed. If you cannot access or transcribe the recording, state that limit and request a transcript. Never invent unheard content.

Keep short source phrases or timestamps where they carry a distinction that a summary could lose. Mark unclear words or uncertain chronology. Do not silently repair ambiguity into a fact.

## Work toward understanding

### 1. Recover the concern

Draft a plain account of what happened, what the user expected, what actually happened, and what is at stake. Name who bears the cost when that matters. A claim about quality, authority, disrespect, wasted effort, or an obligation must survive the summary with its actual force.

Separate connected concerns without forcing them into one decision. Recurrence, repetition, and apparent tangents may reveal a shared problem. Show that connection as a hypothesis. If concerns are independent, keep them separate.

### 2. Separate the account from the explanation

Distinguish what the user reports, what available artifacts establish, what the user concludes, and what you infer. A reported event is not independently verified just because it appears in a transcript.

Make the underlying mismatch explicit. What standard, goal, promise, constraint, or responsibility is being violated? How would the reported behavior produce the consequence the user describes?

Translate a character judgment into the behavior or pattern it points to without discarding the complaint. Do not infer intent from outcome. If trust, respect, or power is itself the issue, examine it directly. Do not require a product defect to make it legitimate.

### 3. Develop and check the working reading

Offer the explanation best supported by the account and show what supports it. Mark the missing link. When a different explanation would change the conclusion, name it and say what would distinguish the readings. Do not manufacture equal weight for weak alternatives.

For agent friction, compare the requested outcome with what the agent actually did. Look at the available instruction, response, artifact, or recurring correction. Keep a wrong technical result distinct from a misunderstanding of scope or an unwanted process. Do not presume the user needs to communicate better.

Use the smallest useful evidence check. It might be reconstructing one incident, comparing a stated goal with a delivered result, reading an existing artifact, or identifying the observation still needed. Explain which claim the check would support, weaken, or leave unresolved. Do not require a ten-minute demonstration or start building a fix just to make the account count.

### 4. Return a working understanding

Write a concise synthesis in the user's language. Include what appears to be the underlying issue, why it matters, the evidence and reasoning behind that reading, and what remains unresolved. Use the [artifact shapes](references/artifact-shapes.md) only when a structured note helps.

When the account is thin or the user is still talking through it, return a provisional reading and one consequential question. Do not make the user fill out a taxonomy or explain everything again. Invite correction of the reading, especially any distinction you may have flattened.

Understanding may be enough for this pass. An unresolved question with a clear way to investigate it is a valid outcome. Do not force a recommendation, owner, deadline, acceptance test, or message.

### 5. Choose what follows when needed

Follow the user's purpose once the issue is clearer. The next step may be further inquiry, a private note, a technical check, a decision, a boundary, or no action yet. Explain how the proposed step follows from the understanding.

Draft a message only when the user asks for communication. Keep its claims within the evidence. Drafting does not authorize sending. Do not start implementation, publication, or persistent knowledge capture solely because the analysis suggests it.

## Keep the inquiry honest

- Preserve anger or blunt language when it carries meaning. Do not turn the account into polite copy before understanding it.
- Do not endorse an insult as a finding or replace a concrete complaint with personality analysis.
- Revise the interpretation when evidence contradicts it. Do not erase the original observation merely because its first explanation was wrong.
- Do not infer trauma, an old wound, or distorted perception from anger. Discuss personal history only when the user brings it in and wants it examined.
- Do not treat uncertainty as a demand that the user withdraw the concern. State what is known and what would change the reading.
- Do not claim that the problem is understood merely because the account now sounds orderly. Keep unresolved contradictions visible.
