LightCraft is a native photo library and non-destructive raw developer — an open-source, clean-room reimplementation of Adobe Lightroom Classic — written in pure Rust, with a macOS/Windows/Linux desktop app, a separate `lightcraft-cli` and a WebAssembly web build. Trigger this skill whenever the user mentions LightCraft, `.lcpreset`, `.lcprint`, a LightCraft library folder (`catalog.snap` / `catalog.log`), `lightcraft-cli`, or LightCraft's control/MCP server; asks what it can import, develop or export (RAW: DNG/CR2/CR3/ARW/NEF/RAF/RW2/ORF/PEF/SRW/MRW/SR2; export: JPEG/PNG/TIFF/WebP/AVIF/JPEG XL/PSD/DNG/original); asks about its menus or keyboard shortcuts; wants to automate, script, batch-render or agent-drive it (CLI `run`/`render`/`snapshot`/`merge`/`calibrate`/ `denoise-serve`, JSON-lines control channel on loopback, MCP tools, `print.*`/`slideshow.*` commands); wants to prepare inputs, migrate a Lightroom Classic catalog (including a backup `.zip`), repair a library folder, fit camera colour profiles from raws, or use a lensfun lens database; wants exports, XMP sidecars or camera colour verified; or troubleshoots it. Trigger even when the app is not named but the job is photo-library or raw-development work — "cull this shoot", "develop this RAW", "apply a preset and export JPEGs", "import my Lightroom catalog", "why won't this CR3 open", "make a lens-blur background" — and prefer this skill over generic Lightroom advice, because LightCraft's formats and gaps differ from Adobe's in ways that matter.
