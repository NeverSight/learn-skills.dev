# Packaging

Packaging is the idea, the title and the thumbnail working as one promise. On YouTube, the click happens before a single second of the video plays, so the video can only be as good as the packaging lets it be.

Many top creators design the package *before* writing the video, and drop ideas that can't be packaged [C; widely described by MrBeast and creator-economy analysts]. Do the same: if you can't make someone want to click, don't make the video yet.

## The floor

1. **One promise.** Title + thumbnail + first frame make one clear promise. Not three.
2. **Title and thumbnail complement each other.** The thumbnail shows, the title tells something the thumbnail doesn't. Never repeat the same words in both [C].
3. **Clarity before cleverness.** A confused viewer scrolls on. A curious one clicks.
4. **Curiosity + clarity.** The viewer knows what the video is about, and doesn't know the answer.
5. **Readable at phone size.** One focal point, high contrast, 0–4 words of thumbnail text [C].
6. **Deliver the promise.** The hook must confirm the click immediately (hand off to `hook-craft`). A package that lies earns clicks and loses viewers.
7. **Front-load the title.** The first few words do the work; long titles get truncated, especially on mobile [C].

## Procedure

1. **Read the idea and profile.** Who's the audience? What do they already know? What would make them stop?
2. **Write the promise** in one sentence: "After watching, you'll know/feel/see ___."
3. **Generate 10+ titles** across angles: the weird fact, the stake, the question, the result, the villain, the personal challenge. Keep each under ~60 characters.
4. **Generate 5+ thumbnail concepts.** For each: focal subject, emotion or action, background, text (if any), and color contrast. Sketch in words; hand to `visual-prompting` or a designer.
5. **Pair them.** Pick the 3 best title + thumbnail pairs that complement each other.
6. **Pressure test** each pair:
   - **Stranger test:** would someone who's never heard of the creator click?
   - **Clarity test:** can you tell what the video is about in one second?
   - **Promise test:** does the first 5 seconds of the planned video confirm it?
   - **Feed test:** imagine it next to ten other thumbnails in the niche. Does it stand out?
7. **Set up the A/B.** If the platform supports testing (YouTube's thumbnail and title tests), pick the two pairs that differ the most, not two near-identical ones.

## Short-form packaging

On Shorts, Reels and TikTok, there's no click. The package is:
- **The first frame:** already moving, already interesting.
- **The on-screen text:** the question or stake in 2–6 words.
- **The caption:** adds context or a second hook; often read after the video starts.
- **The cover image:** matters for profile grids and search, less for the feed.

## Thumbnail design rules [C]

- One subject, big. Faces showing a clear emotion read fast; objects need scale or strangeness.
- Contrast: bright subject on a darker or simpler background. Avoid colors that blend into the platform's interface.
- Text adds something the image can't say, in large type, 0–4 words.
- Visual curiosity: something that doesn't make sense yet (an object where it shouldn't be, a before/after, a number).
- Consistent style across a channel helps recognition. Lock it in the `series-bible`.

## Voice floor

Plain words. Specific nouns. Profanity per profile, but check platform rules before strong words in a title or thumbnail (see `human-writing` dials). Full rules: the `human-writing` skill.
