When catching up on an unfamiliar field, start from generic terms, identify the community’s jargon, key people, and venues (conferences, journals, personal blogs, videos, repositories, lists), then follow citations, links, and authors in a chain‑like (potato‑vine) manner, and drop the original source texts (including papers as well as personal articles or video subtitles) without summarizing them directly into ~/research/<topic>/sources/.

Develop a map (map.md) week by week, and stop when every required claim is attached to the original text or when saturation is reached.

In monitoring mode, fetch the latest items from the map’s venues (Qiita and Zenn tags, note, YouTube, blogs, arXiv) via RSS and list them with the beginnings of the original texts.

Used for the following: “investigate in a chain‑like manner,” “I want to catch up on this field,” “dig from community terms and people,” “sift through papers and prior research,” “look for the original source because I want to imitate existing technology,” “collect the methods of skillful people,” “drop it in the original text,” “/imozuru,” “Is there research on XX? I want the original source,” “I don’t understand at all, so dig the flow,” “I want to know how it’s referred to in the community,” “Check new items of XX,” “Summarize this week’s XX articles,” “catch up,” “/imozuru watch,” “dig into this field,” “find the canonical papers on X,” “literature dive,” “what’s new in X this week.”

Not used for the following: confirming a single factual answer (only year, existence, a single specification → search directly), searching for documents on the local PC, writing summaries or explanations of material that has been read.
