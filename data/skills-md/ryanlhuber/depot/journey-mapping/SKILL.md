# Journey Mapping

## Purpose
Show what a user goes through from first need to outcome across stages and touchpoints, grounded in evidence, so teams see where the experience breaks down and where to focus.

## When to use
- Use when: the experience spans time, channels, or people, including steps before, between, or after using the product.
- Use when: teams disagree about where the experience fails, or have only looked at one touchpoint.
- Do not use when: the question is the step-by-step path through the interface to one goal; use user flow mapping.

## Inputs
- Required: the user type and goal, the start and end of the journey, and evidence about what users do and experience.
- Optional: research synthesis, support or analytics data, known channels and touchpoints, business owners per stage.
- Assumptions: any stage, touchpoint, or feeling not supported by evidence is marked as assumed.

## Process
1. Fix the scope: one user type, one goal, with defined start and end.
2. Define the stages the user passes through, in their terms, not the organization's.
3. For each stage, record the user's actions, the touchpoints and channels involved, and who or what else participates.
4. Record what the user is trying to achieve, thinks, and feels at each stage, only where evidence supports it.
5. Mark pain points and moments that matter most, with the evidence and the consequence for the goal.
6. Identify gaps between stages, handoffs between channels, and points where users leave or work around the system.
7. List opportunities as options tied to specific pain points, and the questions that need further research.

## Rules and heuristics
- Required: label each cell as observed, inferred, or assumed. Do not present assumed emotions as findings.
- Required: one journey per user type and goal. Do not blend different users into an average.
- Recommended: include what happens outside the product when it shapes the experience.
- Recommended: show high-impact moments, not every minor step.

## Checks
- Every stage has evidence or is marked assumed.
- Each pain point links to evidence and a consequence.
- Opportunities map to specific pain points.
- Start, end, user type, and goal are stated.

## Output
Return the scope, the stage-by-stage map, pain points with evidence, cross-channel gaps, opportunities as options, and open research questions. Use the [journey map template](templates/journey-map.md).

## Completion criteria
- The whole journey from start to end is covered or the gaps are stated.
- Observed, inferred, and assumed content are distinguishable.
