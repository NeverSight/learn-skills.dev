# Persona and Scenario Drafting

## Purpose
Draft user personas and usage scenarios grounded in supplied research, with every inference labeled so nothing invented is treated as fact.

## When to use
- Use when: a team needs a shared, concrete picture of users and situations to guide design decisions.
- Use when: research notes, interview summaries, analytics, or support evidence exist to ground the draft.
- Do not use when: no source material exists; produce an assumptions-and-research-needs list instead of fabricated personas.

## Inputs
- Required: source material about users (research notes, interview or survey summaries, analytics, support themes) and the product or task context.
- Optional: segments, market context, accessibility or environmental needs, existing personas to revise.
- Assumptions: all content not traceable to a source is marked as assumption.

## Process
1. Extract recurring user goals, behaviors, contexts, constraints, and pain points from the sources; note how many sources support each.
2. Group users by differences that change design decisions (goals, expertise, frequency, context, abilities), not by demographics alone.
3. Draft one persona per meaningful group: goal, context of use, relevant behaviors, constraints, needs, and success criteria.
4. Label each persona statement as sourced (with reference), inferred, or assumed.
5. Write scenarios: a trigger, the user's goal, the situation and constraints, the steps they attempt, and the outcome they want.
6. Include scenarios involving constraints that could exclude users, such as assistive technology, poor connectivity, interruption, or shared devices.
7. List research gaps and the questions that would validate each assumption.

## Rules and heuristics
- Required: never invent names-with-biographies, quotes, or statistics presented as findings.
- Required: do not include demographic or personal detail unless it changes a design decision and is sourced.
- Recommended: keep personas few; merge groups that would be designed for identically.
- Recommended: write scenarios as situations, not solutions.

## Checks
- Every persona statement is labeled sourced, inferred, or assumed.
- Each persona differs from the others in a way that affects design.
- No fabricated quotations or numbers appear.
- Scenarios include at least one exclusion-risk case.

## Output
Return personas and scenarios with source labels, a list of assumptions with the evidence needed to confirm them, and identified research gaps.

## Completion criteria
- Every persona and scenario traces to sources or is explicitly labeled as assumed.
- Research gaps and validation questions are listed.
