# Interview Guide Writing

## Purpose
Produce a guide another researcher can run consistently to learn about participants' behavior, context, and needs, with questions that elicit real experience instead of opinion or agreement.

## When to use
- Use when: a team needs depth about how people currently do something, what they need, or why, and can speak with representative participants.
- Do not use when: the aim is to evaluate a specific design with tasks; use usability test planning. Do not use to measure how many people hold a view; interviews do not give that.

## Inputs
- Required: the research objectives or the decisions the interviews should inform, and the target participants.
- Optional: existing research, product context, session length, remote or in-person setting, sensitive topics, accessibility needs.
- Assumptions: unspecified participant criteria and session length are stated as assumed.

## Process
1. Restate the objectives as a few research questions the interviews must answer. Drop questions that no decision depends on.
2. Define participant criteria, including who to exclude, and a short screening check.
3. Draft the flow: introduction and consent, warm-up, context, the core topics in a logical order, wrap-up, and thanks.
4. Write open questions about past behavior and specific recent events before attitudes or hypotheticals.
5. Add probes for depth under each core question: example, reason, frequency, what happened next.
6. Rewrite any leading, double-barreled, or hypothetical question into a neutral, single, concrete one.
7. Estimate timing per section and mark what to cut first if time runs short.
8. Add handling notes for sensitive topics and for participants who need a pause or to stop.

## Rules and heuristics
- Required: ask about what people have done, not what they would do. Anchor questions in a specific recent instance.
- Required: obtain informed consent and state how the material will be recorded and used before starting.
- Required: no question may suggest a preferred answer or name the proposed solution.
- Recommended: put the most important topics early, as time and attention drop later.
- Recommended: write questions in the participants' vocabulary, not internal terms.

## Checks
- Every research question has at least one core question, and every core question serves a research question.
- No question is leading, double-barreled, or hypothetical without a reason stated.
- The timing fits the stated session length.
- Consent and sensitive-topic handling are present.

## Output
Return the research questions, participant criteria and screener, the guide with timings, probes, a list of questions rewritten for neutrality, and notes for the interviewer.

## Completion criteria
- Another researcher could run the interview from the guide alone.
- Each research question is covered and the guide fits the session length.
