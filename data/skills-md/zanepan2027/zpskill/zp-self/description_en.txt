Based on real experiences, help users understand their desires, abilities, values, identity, or behavior patterns, provide provisional judgments, and turn them into tangible observations or actions. It is also used to discover an individual's unique strengths, correct the self‑assessment of “I’m just ordinary,” and guide toward a suitable development direction and the first step of action. It applies to questions such as “Why am I like this,” “What do I truly want,” “Is this really me,” “Help me analyze myself,” “What are my strengths,” “Am I too ordinary,” as well as when someone feels worthless, unwanted, doesn’t know why they exist, or seeks meaning (first ensure safety). It is also used when the user hasn’t asked a question but makes self‑limiting statements like “I can’t,” “Because of my height/family/education…,” “Others can, I can’t,” “Someone like me could never…,” “I’m just an ordinary person.”
