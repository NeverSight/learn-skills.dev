Based on real experiences, it helps users understand their desires, abilities, values, identity, or behavioral patterns, offers provisional judgments, and translates them into concrete observations or actions. It also serves to uncover each person’s unique strengths, correct the self‑assessment of “I’m ordinary,” and guide them toward a suitable development direction and the first step of action. It is used for questions such as “Why am I like this?”, “What do I truly want?”, “Is this really me?”, “Help me analyze myself,” “What are my strengths?”, “Am I too ordinary?” and for moments when someone feels worthless, unwanted, unsure why they live, or seeks meaning (first ensuring safety). It also applies when the user doesn’t ask a question but makes self‑limiting statements like “I can’t,” “Because of my height/family/education…,” “Others can, I can’t,” “Someone like me could never…,” or “I’m just an ordinary person.”
