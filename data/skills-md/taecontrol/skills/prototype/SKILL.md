# Prototype

Use when exercising a product behavior, interaction, state model, or UI alternative can settle a design question. A prototype is discovery-only, never a delivery phase.

## Process

1. State one design question, why it affects the next decision, intended users and constraints, the actions and observations that can settle it, and a cost, time, or scope bound.
2. Before building, choose one disposition: delete after preserving the evidence, or retain only in an isolated discovery location excluded from production candidates.
3. Build only the behavior needed to make the question observable. Use a state or logic artifact for transitions, and a visual artifact for presentation or interaction.
4. Exercise representative actions and important edge or failure states. Capture the observations.
5. Render and inspect every visual artifact in its intended runtime before presentation. Capture rendered evidence for every presented variant or state.
6. Return to the Coordinator the evidence, material surprises, supported verdict, limits, and recommendation. Do not update the goal map, routing, or accepted decisions.

Any reuse, copying, or adaptation begins an explicit production promotion. It needs a Coordinator-recorded production slice and re-entry through the complete production delivery lifecycle. When promotion makes a material decision, obtain explicit human acceptance first.

## Completion criteria

- The question, decision relevance, settling observations, and bound are explicit.
- The artifact exposes the needed behavior, states, or alternatives.
- Representative and edge or failure states produced captured observations.
- Every presented visual artifact was rendered, inspected, and evidenced.
- The artifact has one explicit disposable disposition.
- Retention does not imply promotion, and reuse is identified as an explicit production promotion.
