# Manage Product Portfolio

## Contract

Portfolio management allocates scarce attention and capacity to a system of choices. Do not rank unlike work in one score or let committed work, maintenance, risk, and exploratory options disappear behind feature demand.

## Workflow

1. Define portfolio boundary, strategy, outcomes, horizon, capacity, constraints, decision rights, and review cadence.
2. Inventory active and candidate investments with owner, segment, outcome, lifecycle stage, evidence, spend or capacity, dependencies, commitments, and next decision.
3. Normalize work into lanes such as obligations, core health, growth, new bets, discovery, platform capability, and sunset while preserving strategic differences.
4. Identify duplication, gaps, concentration, hidden shared dependencies, unsupported commitments, and initiatives with no credible path to an outcome.
5. Evaluate portfolio balance across horizons, segments, risk, reversibility, evidence maturity, return profile, learning, and operational load.
6. Allocate bounded capacity and funding. Sequence dependencies and protect deliberate discovery, maintenance, reliability, compliance, and transition work.
7. Decide invest, continue, reshape, combine, hold, or stop for each material item with rationale, consequences, and change triggers.
8. Establish a portfolio review using exact states, outcome evidence, capacity variance, risk, and decisions rather than aggregated status reporting.

## BuildBetter Acceleration

If BuildBetter MCP is available, search organization skills first. Use project types, projects, triage, linked tickets, customer evidence, surveys, documents, and prior decisions to build the portfolio view. Promotion or project mutation requires explicit approval.

## Output

Return portfolio inventory, allocation principles, lane and horizon view, concentration and dependency findings, capacity allocation, item decisions, deferrals, stop list, risks, and review cadence.

## Quality Gate

Reject portfolios that fund every strategy, compare obligations directly with speculative bets, omit maintenance or sunset work, hide capacity, or continue initiatives solely because they have already consumed investment.
