# Define Product Positioning

## Contract

Positioning defines the context in which a specific customer understands the product's value. Start from what customers would use otherwise. Keep positioning separate from copywriting.

## Workflow

1. Select the best-fit customer and the high-value situation or job.
2. List the actual alternatives, including manual processes, adjacent tools, services, and non-consumption.
3. Identify capabilities or attributes that are credibly different from those alternatives.
4. Translate each difference into customer value and a concrete proof point.
5. Choose the market category or frame that makes the value easiest to understand without creating false expectations.
6. Draft the positioning: for whom, in what situation, what category, what value, unlike which alternative, because of what proof.
7. Test with customer language, objections, loss reasons, comprehension, and sales or onboarding behavior. Record where positioning varies by segment.

## BuildBetter Acceleration

If BuildBetter MCP is available, search organization skills first. Retrieve competitor mentions, objections, alternatives, feature requests, call evidence, survey responses, and account context. Keep internal enablement claims separate from external customer language.

## Output

Return best-fit segment, alternatives, differentiated attributes, value mapping, proof, category frame, positioning statement, message implications, objections, and tests.

## Quality Gate

Reject generic value, unproved superiority, audience sprawl, and invented categories that require more explanation than the product. Every differentiator must connect to customer value.
