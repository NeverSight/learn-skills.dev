The academic writing and topic-argumentation workstation for social sciences and related fields, covering the four key elements: topic conception, literature review, argument organization, and scholarly expression. Use for finding thesis titles/research questions, turning interests or broad topics into discussable research directions, literature reviews and field mapping, distinguishing “little‑researched” from truly unresolved knowledge problems, designing evidence pathways and claim boundaries, novelty audits and “Reviewer 2”‑style attacks, evaluating doctoral/dissertation feasibility, mentor communication scripts, or checking for overstatements and conceptual mixing in papers. Also applicable to the topic‑selection stage and self‑checking of arguments in proposal reports, research plans, and dissertations.
