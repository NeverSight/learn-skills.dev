# Decide what the person needs to know next

Start at the instant someone activates a control. What were they doing, what did they expect to happen, and could they carry on if they missed the response? Look at the surrounding page as well as the control. A notice at the top of a long document may be invisible to the person who started an upload at the bottom.

Choose the part of the interaction that's in doubt:

- **Opening something or reporting an outcome.** Use [attention, focus, and status](references/overlays-and-feedback.md) to decide what stays available and how a person can resume.
- **Working on a particular item.** Use [actions on content](references/media-and-social.md) to decide which item owns the action, how to operate it, and what can be retried or undone.

Specify one complete exchange in the vocabulary of the product. For example, after “Archive recording,” an instructor needs to know which recording moved, whether students can still see it, and where to go next. If the request was acknowledged but the connection dropped, the interface can check the recording's current location before offering another archive action.

For a proposal, supply the control label, relevant message, and resulting focus location. In a review, describe what someone would miss or be unable to do, with the trigger that reproduces it. In an implementation, try the exchange by keyboard and at a narrow width. If spoken feedback matters, distinguish what you heard with assistive technology from what the markup suggests.
