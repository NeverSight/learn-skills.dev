# Consulting Deck Expert

Turn a business question into a deck that helps a specific audience make a decision. A polished template is not a substitute for a clear recommendation, defensible evidence, and a coherent path from problem to action.

## Core contract

1. Preserve the user's facts, approved wording, brand rules, and confidentiality constraints.
2. Never invent evidence, customer claims, benchmarks, citations, or financial values. Mark gaps as assumptions or data requests.
3. Lead with the answer when the evidence supports one. If it does not, present the decision criteria and what must be learned next.
4. Give each slide one governing message. The title should state that message; the body should prove it.
5. Make the deck skimmable at title level and defensible at exhibit level.
6. Keep charts, diagrams, and text editable whenever the authoring format allows it.

## Choose the mode

- **New deck:** define the decision brief, build a message tree, then create the slide map.
- **Restructure:** extract the current title storyline, diagnose gaps or repetition, and rebuild the sequence before polishing individual slides.
- **Executive polish:** preserve the approved narrative while strengthening titles, evidence hierarchy, exhibit choice, and visual density.
- **Proposal:** connect client context, diagnosis, approach, proof, workplan, team, and commercial next step without turning the deck into a capabilities catalog.

## Required workflow

### 1. Write the decision brief

Capture the audience, decision, stakes, current belief, desired belief, meeting context, evidence available, and hard constraints. If the user has not supplied all of these, infer only low-risk items and label the rest.

### 2. Build the answer tree

Write one governing thought and 2–4 supporting arguments. Use SCQA for the opening and pyramid logic for the body. Use an issue tree only when the branches are decision-relevant and collectively cover the problem.

Read [references/storyline.md](references/storyline.md) when creating or materially restructuring the narrative.

### 3. Create the slide map

Before drawing, produce a compact map:

| # | Action title | Evidence / exhibit | Decision role | Source status |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-sentence governing thought | Hero fact or synthesis | Orient | verified |
| 2 | First supporting conclusion | Chart, table, or customer evidence | Prove | verified / gap |

The title sequence should read as a coherent executive summary without opening the body of any slide.

### 4. Match each claim to an exhibit

Choose the simplest visual form that proves the claim. Prefer charts for quantitative comparison, diagrams for systems or flows, tables for precise lookup, and short prose for judgment or implication. Do not turn every idea into a collection of cards.

Read [references/exhibit-patterns.md](references/exhibit-patterns.md) when selecting charts, matrices, processes, timelines, or architecture diagrams.

### 5. Design the slide

- Use a conclusion-led title, usually one or two lines.
- Reserve the largest area for the proof, not for decoration.
- Use 2–4 visual groups on a normal content slide.
- Align repeated elements to a grid and keep comparable items visually comparable.
- Use color to encode meaning or emphasis, not to fill empty space.
- Put sources and assumptions close to the evidence they qualify.
- Move detail that does not support the decision to an appendix.

### 6. Close the argument

Translate findings into choices, recommendation, impact, risks, owners, timing, and next decisions. A roadmap should show dependencies and decision gates, not only a row of calendar boxes.

### 7. Red-team the deck

Read [references/qa-checklist.md](references/qa-checklist.md). Test whether every conclusion is supported, alternatives were considered, numbers reconcile, titles form a story, and the final ask is explicit.

## Writing rules

- Prefer specific verbs and nouns over consulting filler.
- Distinguish fact, interpretation, assumption, and recommendation.
- Use parallel grammar for sibling points.
- Replace topic titles such as “Market Overview” with claims such as “Two segments account for most near-term growth.”
- Keep labels short; put nuance in annotations, notes, or appendix pages.
- Do not shrink text to rescue an overloaded slide. Split the argument or simplify the exhibit.

## Delivery

Deliver the deck plus a short audit note covering the governing thought, title storyline, unresolved evidence gaps, assumptions, and any pages that need executive confirmation.
