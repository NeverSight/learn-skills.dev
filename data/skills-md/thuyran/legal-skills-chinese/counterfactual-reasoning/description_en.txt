When it is necessary to assess “how legal outcomes would differ if a certain fact, act, or condition were absent or changed,” this skill is triggered. Typical scenarios include: determining causation (whether damage would still occur without the act), allocating fault and liability proportions, defining the scope of damages, calculating contract breach losses, testing the legality of administrative actions, judging interruptions in criminal causation, and evaluating legislative impacts. The core method involves constructing a “counterfactual world” that differs from reality only in the target variable, comparing the results of the two worlds, and thereby determining the contribution of the target variable to the outcome.
